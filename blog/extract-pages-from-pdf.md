# How to Extract Pages from a PDF Online

Sometimes you don't need an entire PDF document — just one chapter, a single form, or a few specific pages. Instead of sending the whole file or asking someone to scroll through dozens of pages to find what matters, you can extract exactly the pages you need and save them as a new, smaller PDF.

## Why Extract Pages from a PDF?

- **Share only relevant content**: Send just the pages someone needs instead of an entire report or book.
- **Create smaller, focused documents**: Pull out a single form, certificate, or chapter from a larger combined file.
- **Simplify reviews**: When asking a colleague to review one section of a document, extracting that section makes their job easier.
- **Reduce file size**: A smaller extracted document is easier to email, upload, or store.

## How to Extract Pages from a PDF Online

1. **Open the page extraction tool** in your browser.
2. **Upload your PDF file** from your device or cloud storage.
3. **View the page thumbnails** of your document.
4. **Select the pages** you want to extract — you can choose individual pages, a range (e.g., pages 5-10), or multiple non-consecutive pages.
5. **Extract the selected pages** into a new PDF.
6. **Download the new file**, which contains only the pages you selected.

## Extract vs. Split — What's the Difference?

These two features are related but serve slightly different purposes:

- **Extracting pages** creates a single new PDF containing only the pages you select — useful when you want one specific section as its own document.
- **Splitting a PDF** divides the entire document into multiple smaller files, often based on page ranges — useful when you want to break a large document into several parts, each potentially used separately.

If you only need one portion of a document, extraction is usually the simpler choice. If you need to divide an entire document into several pieces, splitting is more appropriate.

## Common Use Cases

- **Students**: Extracting a single chapter or worksheet from a textbook PDF for focused study.
- **HR teams**: Pulling out a signed signature page from a longer contract for record-keeping.
- **Accountants**: Extracting specific invoice pages from a combined statement for client records.
- **Legal professionals**: Separating exhibits or appendices from a larger legal document for individual reference.

## Tips for Extracting Pages

- **Double-check page numbers**: PDF viewers sometimes number pages differently than the printed numbers within the document (for example, if there's an unnumbered cover page) — verify you're selecting the correct pages before extracting.
- **Preview before downloading**: Always preview the extracted pages to confirm you got exactly what you needed.
- **Rename extracted files clearly**: Give your extracted PDF a descriptive name (e.g., "Invoice-March-Page3.pdf") so it's easy to identify later.

## Is It Safe to Extract Pages Online?

Online PDF tools process your file temporarily to extract the selected pages and generate a new document for download. Files are typically removed from the server after processing. For documents containing sensitive information, use a tool with secure (HTTPS) connections.

## Final Thoughts

Extracting pages from a PDF is a quick way to pull out exactly the content you need without dealing with an entire document. With a free online tool, you can select individual pages or ranges and create a new, focused PDF in just a few clicks — perfect for sharing, reviewing, or organizing your files.
